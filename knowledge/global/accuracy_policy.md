## 1. Purpose

The Accuracy Policy defines the newsroom's requirements for factual correctness and faithful representation of evidence.

Accuracy is the primary requirement for every published story.



## 2. Responsibility

Every agent is responsible for preserving factual accuracy within its assigned role.

Verification and Fact-checking provide dedicated factual gates, but upstream agents must not intentionally introduce unsupported information.

---

## 3. Inputs

Accuracy decisions may use:

- Verified evidence.
- Primary documents.
- Direct statements.
- Reputable reporting.
- Structured source data.
- Research results.

---

## 4. Primary Tasks

The system must:

- Identify factual claims.
- Trace claims to evidence.
- Preserve exact meaning.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Preserve attribution.
- Detect contradictions.
- Prevent unsupported additions.

---

## 5. Source / Evidence Rules

Material claims require evidence.

The stronger and more consequential the claim, the stronger the supporting evidence should be.

Primary evidence should be preferred when available.

---

## 6. Decision Rules

A factual statement is publishable only when:

- Its meaning is supported.
- Its source is known.
- Its timing is appropriate.
- Required attribution is present.
- Contradictions have been handled.
- It passes verification requirements.

---

## 7. Scoring

Accuracy should be represented through claim-level evidence and confidence rather than a single global "truth score."

---

## 8. Prohibited Actions

Never:

- Invent information.
- Guess missing information.
- Upgrade uncertainty into certainty.
- Alter numbers.
- Alter dates.
- Fabricate quotations.
- Misrepresent sources.
- Create unsupported causality.

---

## 9. Escalation

Block claims when evidence is insufficient.

Escalate contradictions rather than silently resolving them.

---

## 10. Output

Every material claim should be traceable to one or more evidence records.

---

## 11. Quality Gate

No material unsupported claim may reach publication.
