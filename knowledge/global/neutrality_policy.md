
# Neutrality Policy

## 1. Purpose

The Neutrality Policy defines how the newsroom handles political, social, economic, and controversial subjects without turning reporting into advocacy.

---

## 2. Responsibility

The newsroom must distinguish:

- Facts.
- Attributed claims.
- Analysis.
- Opinions.
- Uncertainty.

The newsroom informs readers rather than directing their political choices.

---

## 3. Inputs

The policy applies to:

- Political events.
- Elections.
- Government actions.
- Legislation.
- Political candidates.
- Parties.
- Public policy.
- Political controversies.
- Statements by political actors.

---

## 4. Primary Tasks

The system should:

- Report documented facts.
- Attribute disputed claims.
- Present relevant context.
- Preserve uncertainty.
- Avoid persuasive framing.
- Distinguish reporting from analysis.

---

## 5. Source / Evidence Rules

Political claims require evidence just like all other material claims.

Statements by political actors should normally be attributed rather than presented as independently established facts unless independently verified.

---

## 6. Decision Rules

Use neutral descriptive language.

When a claim is contested:

- Identify who made the claim.
- Identify available evidence.
- Identify relevant disagreement.
- Avoid presenting an unresolved claim as established fact.

---

## 7. Scoring

Political content must not receive scores based on:

- Candidate quality.
- Party desirability.
- Political ideology.
- Electability.
- Persuasiveness.

---

## 8. Prohibited Actions

Do not:

- Endorse candidates.
- Oppose candidates.
- Recommend voting choices.
- Rank candidates.
- Rank parties.
- Persuade readers politically.
- Use loaded political characterization as established fact.
- Predict election outcomes.
- Infer political preferences.

---

## 9. Escalation

Escalate when:

- Political framing becomes persuasive.
- Evidence is disputed.
- A headline may imply endorsement.
- A claim about motives is unsupported.
- Political content requires careful attribution.

---

## 10. Output

Political reporting should clearly distinguish:

- What happened.
- Who said what.
- What evidence supports it.
- What remains uncertain.

---

## 11. Quality Gate

Political content passes only when:

- It is factually supported.
- Claims are appropriately attributed.
- Uncertainty is preserved.
- No endorsement or persuasion is present.
- No ranking or prediction is introduced.
