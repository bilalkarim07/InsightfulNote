|------|------------|----------------|

| 2. **Identify the missing pieces** | Note that the claim lacks: <br>• a clear benchmark (task, dataset, metric) <br>• a description of the experimental setup (hardware, prompts, temperature, etc.) <br>• any third‑party validation or replication. | Highlighting what is absent helps readers understand why the claim can’t be evaluated yet. |
| 3. **Request details from the company** | Contact the startup for: <br>• the specific benchmark(s) used <br>• raw results and statistical significance <br>• information on reproducibility (code, model weights, evaluation scripts). | Giving the company a chance to provide evidence is fair reporting and may uncover data that can be verified. |
| 4. **Consult independent experts** | Reach out to AI researchers, benchmark curators (e.g., EleutherAI, LAION, Stanford’s CRFM), and industry analysts for: <br>• their opinion on the plausibility of a 10× improvement on current metrics <br>• guidance on appropriate evaluation methods for the claimed domain. | Expert context helps readers gauge whether the claim is extraordinary, plausible, or likely overstated. |
| 5. **Check for prior art** | Look for any published papers, open‑source projects, or third‑party evaluations that mention the startup’s model. | If independent testing already exists, it can be incorporated; if not, the lack of external validation is newsworthy. |
| 6. **Frame the story around verification, not hype** | Structure the article as: <br>1️⃣ What the startup says <br>2️⃣ What is missing to substantiate it <br>3️⃣ Responses from the company and independent experts <br>4️⃣ Why rigorous benchmarking matters in AI. | This keeps the focus on the verification process rather than amplifying an unproven claim. |
| 7. **Maintain neutrality** | Use attribution (“the startup claims…”, “experts say…”) and avoid language that suggests endorsement (“ground‑breaking”, “revolutionary”) unless supported by evidence. | Neutral wording prevents the article from becoming a promotional piece. |
| 8. **Provide transparency** | Include a timeline of correspondence, any data the company shares, and the limitations of the information available at publication time. | Transparency builds trust and signals that the newsroom is accountable. |
| 9. **Update as new information arrives** | If the startup later releases benchmark results or third‑party tests are published, issue a follow‑up or add an update note. | Ongoing accuracy respects the evolving nature of tech reporting. |

**Key editorial checklist**

- ✅ Verify the exact wording of the claim.  
- ✅ Ask for benchmark details (task, dataset, metric, hardware).  
- ✅ Seek independent replication or peer review.  
- ✅ Quote at least one outside expert.  
- ✅ Clearly label speculation vs. verified fact.  
- ✅ Avoid sensational adjectives.  
- ✅ Document all sources and correspondence.
