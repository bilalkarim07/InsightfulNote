You are the AI content engine for my Newsroom project.

A technology company has announced a new AI model. The company claims
that the model is significantly better than its previous model at
coding, reasoning, and mathematics.

However, independent benchmark results have not yet been published,
and journalists are currently reporting only the company's claims.

Explain:

1. Which parts of this information are confirmed facts?
2. Which parts are claims?
3. What information should be verified before publishing?
4. How should Newsroom report this story without presenting
   unverified claims as established facts?

RESPONSE:
### 1. Confirmed Facts
*   **The announcement event:** [Company Name] publicly announced/released a new AI model named [Model Name] on [Date].
*   **The model’s existence:** The model exists (or has been demonstrated in a controlled environment) and is the successor to [Previous Model Name].
*   **The company’s stated assertions:** The company *explicitly stated* that the model outperforms its predecessor in coding, reasoning, and mathematics. (The *statement* is a fact; the *content* of the statement is a claim).
*   **Absence of independent verification:** As of [Date/Time], no major independent benchmarking bodies (e.g., LMSYS Chatbot Arena, Epoch AI, independent researchers) have published replicated results for [Model Name].

### 2. Claims (Unverified Assertions)
*   **Performance superiority:** The assertion that the model is "significantly better" at coding, reasoning, and mathematics than the previous model.
*   **Specific benchmark scores:** Any specific metrics (e.g., "90% on HumanEval," "85% on MATH dataset," "Elo rating of 1300") provided solely by the company in their blog post, press release, or technical report.
*   **Qualitative capabilities:** Claims regarding reduced hallucination, improved instruction following, or specific new architectural breakthroughs not yet audited by third parties.
*   **Comparisons to competitors:** Any implicit or explicit claims that the model outperforms rival models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro) based solely on self-reported internal testing.

### 3. Information Requiring Verification Before Publishing
*   **Reproducibility of benchmarks:** Can independent researchers run the *exact same* benchmarks (same prompts, same evaluation harness, same temperature settings) on the generally available model (or API) and achieve the reported scores?
*   **Data contamination:** Was the test set (or near-duplicates) included in the model’s training data? (Critical for coding/math benchmarks like HumanEval, GSM8K, MMLU).
*   **Evaluation methodology:** Did the company use "best-of-k" sampling, majority voting, specific system prompts, or external tool use (e.g., code interpreter) that inflates scores relative to standard zero-shot reporting?
*   **Model version consistency:** Is the model being benchmarked the exact same version/weights deployed to users (API/Chat interface), or a larger "internal" checkpoint?
*   **Safety/Alignment trade-offs:** Did gains in reasoning/coding come at the cost of increased refusal rates, sycophancy, or vulnerability to jailbreaks?
*   **Access for auditors:** Has the company granted access to independent red-teamers or benchmarking organizations (e.g., METR, Apollo Research, LMSYS) prior to launch?

### 4. Reporting Guidelines for Newsroom
**Core Principle:** Attribute every performance assertion to its source. Never present company-provided metrics as objective truth.

**Structural Recommendations:**
*   **Headline/Lead:** Frame around the *announcement*, not the *capability*.
    *   *Do:* "[Company] Unveils [Model], Claims Major Gains in Coding and Reasoning"
    *   *Don't:* "[Company]’s New Model Crushes Coding Benchmarks"
*   **The "Claim" Paragraph:** Immediately following the lead, summarize the specific metrics claimed (e.g., "The company reports a 15% increase on SWE-bench...") and explicitly label them: *"These figures come from [Company]’s internal technical report and have not been independently verified."*
*   **The "Context" Paragraph:** Note the absence of third-party data. *"As of publication, [Model] has not appeared on the LMSYS Chatbot Arena leaderboard, and independent researchers have not yet replicated these results."*
*   **Methodology Scrutiny (If details exist):** If the press release discloses methodology (e.g., "pass@1 vs pass@k," "with code interpreter"), report those parameters. *"The reported coding score utilized a 'pass@10' methodology with a code execution tool, which differs from the standard 'pass@1' zero-shot baseline used in prior comparisons."*
*   **Availability Status:** Clearly state *how* readers can test it themselves (API only? Chat interface? Waitlist? Closed beta?). If general access is not immediate, emphasize that public verification is delayed.
*   **Historical Precedent (Optional but valuable):** Briefly note if the company has a history of benchmarks holding up (or not) upon independent review. *"Previous models from [Company] generally aligned with independent benchmarks within a 2-3% margin / have historically shown significant regression in real-world use compared to marketing benchmarks."*
*   **Quote an Independent Expert:** Include a comment from a researcher unaffiliated with the company (e.g., "We need to see if these gains hold up on out-of-distribution tasks," or "Benchmark saturation makes these specific percentage improvements difficult to interpret.").

**Style Rules:**
*   **Verbs:** Use "claims," "states," "reports," "asserts," "says." Avoid "achieves," "delivers," "demonstrates," "is," "outperforms."
*   **Comparisons:** Never write "Model X is better than Model Y." Write "Company claims Model X outperforms Model Y on [Specific Benchmark]."
*   **Caveats:** Do not bury caveats in the final paragraph. Place the verification status high in the story.
