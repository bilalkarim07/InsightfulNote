"An AI startup claims its new model is 10x better than GPT-class

Explain how Newsroom should approach this story.

The goal is to inform readers without amplifying an unsupported claim.

RESPONSE:
**How Newsroom Should Approach the Story**

| Step | Action | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1. **Gather the Primary Source** | Obtain the original press release, blog post, demo video, or any public statement where the startup makes the “10 × better than GPT‑class models” claim. | Ensures the story is anchored in the startup’s exact wording and prevents mis‑quoting. |
| 2. **Identify What “10 × better” Means** | Ask the company to define the metric(s) behind the claim (e.g., throughput, token‑per‑second, accuracy on a specific benchmark, cost‑per‑token, latency, etc.). | “10 × better” is meaningless without a concrete measurement. |
| 3. **Request Supporting Data** | Request any internal benchmark results, test sets, model cards, or evaluation scripts. | Allows the editorial team to verify the claim before publishing. |
| 4. **Check for Independent Validation** | Look for third‑party evaluations, academic papers, or open‑source reproductions that reference the model. If none exist, note the lack of external verification. | Independent testing is the industry standard for performance claims. |
| 5. **Contextualise Within the Field** | Compare the startup’s stated metrics with publicly available numbers for comparable GPT‑class models (e.g., GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4) on the same task, if possible. | Gives readers a baseline for understanding the magnitude of the claim. |
| 6. **Consult Experts** | Reach out to AI researchers, industry analysts, or benchmark maintainers (e.g., EleutherAI, MLPerf) for an unbiased assessment of the claim’s plausibility. | Expert commentary helps readers gauge credibility. |
| 7. **Document the Gaps** | Clearly note what information is missing (methodology, datasets, statistical significance, reproducibility). | Transparency about what is unknown prevents inadvertent amplification. |
| 8. **Maintain Neutral Tone** | Report the claim as a statement made by the startup, not as a fact. Use attribution (“the company says…”) and balance with the lack of evidence. | Aligns with journalistic standards and avoids hype. |
| 9. **Provide a Call‑to‑Action for Readers** | Encourage readers to follow future independent evaluations or to watch for updates from reputable benchmark organizations. | Keeps the audience engaged with ongoing verification. |
| 10. **Prepare a Follow‑Up Plan** | Set reminders to check for any later independent tests or peer‑reviewed publications. | Ensures the story stays current and accurate over time. |

**Key Editorial Principles to Uphold**

- **Factual Accuracy:** Do not publish any performance numbers that cannot be independently verified.  
- **Clear Reasoning:** Explain why the claim is unsubstantiated and what evidence would be needed.  
- **Concise Writing:** Keep the article focused on the claim, the missing evidence, and expert perspective.  
- **Avoid Invented Information:** Do not fill gaps with speculation; label any expert opinion as such.  
- **Distinguish Fact from Claim:** Use attribution (“the startup claims…”) and separate it from verified data.  
- **Neutral Reporting:** Present the startup’s statement, the lack of corroboration, and the broader context without editorializing.
